## Corruption Typologies

### Common Schemes

#### 1. APBD Markups
**Method**: Inflating budget items above market price
**Example**: Road construction budgeted at Rp 5M/km (market: Rp 2M/km)
**Detection**: Compare with standard unit prices (HPS)

#### 2. Ghost Projects
**Method**: Budget allocated for non-existent projects
**Example**: "Community training" with no attendees or materials
**Detection**: Physical verification, beneficiary interviews

#### 3. Related-Party Transactions
**Method**: Politician's company wins government contracts
**Example**: Governor's wife's company wins all catering contracts
**Detection**: Cross-reference company ownership with tender winners

#### 4. Kickbacks
**Method**: Contractor pays percentage to official
**Example**: 10% of contract value returned as cash
**Detection**: Lifestyle analysis, bank account monitoring

#### 5. License Trading
**Method**: Selling permits for mining, logging, plantations
**Example**: Governor issues permit to company in exchange for shares
**Detection**: Track permit issuance timing vs. ownership changes

## Legal Framework

### Anti-Corruption Laws

**UU 31/1999 jo UU 20/2001**:
- Defines corruption crimes
- Penalties: 4 years to death penalty
- Asset recovery provisions

**UU 19/2019 (KPK Law)**:
- Establishes KPK powers
- Investigation, prosecution, prevention
- Controversial revisions in 2019

**UU 27/2022 (PDP Law)**:
- Personal data protection
- Impacts investigation methods
- Consent requirements

### Law Enforcement Agencies

**KPK (Komisi Pemberantasan Korupsi)**:
- Independent anti-corruption commission
- Handles cases >Rp 1B or involving officials
- Known for high conviction rate (>95%)

**Kejaksaan Agung**:
- Attorney General's Office
- Prosecutes corruption cases
- Handles asset recovery

**Kepolisian (POLRI)**:
- National police
- Investigates corruption (lower profile cases)
- Often collaborates with KPK

## Key Institutions

### Election Commission (KPU)

**Functions**:
- Organizes elections
- Maintains candidate database
- Publishes election results

**Data Available**:
- Candidate lists (all levels)
- Vote counts by region
- Party seat allocation

**Website**: kpu.go.id

### Wealth Declaration Agency (LHKPN)

**Functions**:
- Collects asset declarations
- Public officials required to report
- Data partially public

**Declaration Requirements**:
- Assets (cash, property, vehicles, investments)
- Liabilities
- Income sources

**Website**: elhkpn.kpk.go.id

### Business Registry (AHU)

**Functions**:
- Company registration
- Shareholder records
- Board member listings

**Data Available**:
- Company name, NPWB, address
- Capital structure
- Directors and commissioners
- Shareholders (for PT)

**Website**: ahuk-online.go.id

### Procurement Portal (LPSE)

**Functions**:
- Government tender announcements
- Bidder information
- Contract awards

**Data Available**:
- Tender documents
- Bidder lists
- Winner announcements
- Contract values

**Website**: lpse.kemenkeu.go.id (national portal)

## Investigation Techniques

### Open Source Intelligence (OSINT)

#### Social Media Analysis
- Facebook: Political posts, connections
- Instagram: Lifestyle, associations
- Twitter/X: Political statements, networks
- TikTok: Campaign content, youth outreach

#### Corporate Records Research
1. Search AHU for company name
2. Extract shareholder list
3. Cross-reference with politician names
4. Check for proxy patterns (multiple companies, same address)

#### Land Registry Search
- BPN (Badan Pertanahan Nasional)
- Property ownership records
- Transaction history
- Often reveals undeclared assets

### Financial Investigation

#### Bank Account Analysis
- Requires court order
- Track large transactions
- Identify shell companies
- Follow money trails

#### Lifestyle Audits
- Compare declared income vs. visible wealth
- Property holdings
- Vehicle ownership
- Travel patterns
- School fees (international schools = red flag)

#### Tax Records
- NPWP verification
- Tax returns (SPT)
- Withholding taxes
- VAT payments

## Notable Cases (Reference)

### E-KTP Case (2017)
**Defendant**: Setya Novanto (ex-DPR Speaker)
**Loss**: Rp 2.3 trillion
**Sentence**: 15 years prison
**Scheme**: Budget markup for electronic ID card project

### Jiwasraya Case (2020)
**Defendants**: Multiple executives, officials
**Loss**: Rp 17 trillion
**Sentence**: Various (up to life imprisonment)
**Scheme**: Investment fraud, fake products

### Asabri Case (2021)
**Defendants**: Fund managers, officials
**Loss**: Rp 23 trillion
**Sentence**: Life imprisonment for main defendants
**Scheme**: Similar to Jiwasraya, mismanagement of pension fund

## Glossary

### Indonesian Terms

**Anggaran**: Budget  
**APBD**: Regional budget (provincial/regency)  
**APBN**: National budget  
**Bupati**: Regent (head of regency)  
**DPR**: National legislature  
**DPRD**: Regional legislature  
**Gubernur**: Governor  
**Gratifikasi**: Gratification (bribes/gifts)  
**KKN**: Korupsi, Kolusi, Nepotisme (Corruption, Collusion, Nepotism)  
**LHKPN**: Wealth declaration report  
**NPWP**: Tax ID number  
**Perda**: Regional regulation  
**Suap**: Bribe  
**Tender**: Procurement bid  
**Walikota**: Mayor  

### English Terms

**Beneficial Owner**: True owner behind nominee  
**Conflict of Interest**: Situation where personal interest conflicts with duty  
**Ghost Project**: Non-existent project with budget allocation  
**Kickback**: Illegal payment for favorable treatment  
**Money Laundering**: Concealing origins of illegally obtained money  
**Oligarchy**: Small group controlling government/policy  
**Political Dynasty**: Family maintaining political power across generations  
**Procurement Fraud**: Manipulation of government purchasing process  
**Shell Company**: Company with no significant assets/operations  
**State Loss**: Financial damage to government from corruption
